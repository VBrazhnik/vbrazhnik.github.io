Privacy Policy

Last updated: September 17, 2026

This Privacy Policy applies to the Android application “obleek” (package name: com.vbrazhnik.obleek), available on Google Play (“the App”), developed by Vladyslav Brazhnik (“the Developer”). It explains how the App handles information when you use it.

Summary

obleek is a personal budget and transaction tracker. Your financial data — accounts, transactions, categories, plans, tags, and notes — stays in a local database on your device. The App has no account system and no server of its own, so the Developer never receives your financial data. The App contains no advertising and requests no advertising identifier.

The App uses a small number of third-party services, each described in its own section below: public exchange-rate providers, optional and opt-in usage analytics, on-device text recognition used by the optional transaction-detection feature, and the payment services behind the optional obleek Pro subscription.

Information the App Accesses

The accounts, transactions, categories, recurring plans, tags, and notes you create are stored only in a local database on your device, so the App can display and manage them. This information is never read by anyone but you, and is never uploaded, logged, or sent anywhere by the Developer.

Exchange Rate Data

To convert between currencies and show approximate values for BTC/ETH balances, the App periodically fetches public exchange rates from two third-party rate providers, ExchangeRate-API and CoinGecko. These requests only ask for a currency or ticker code (for example, “USD” or “BTC”) — no account data, transaction data, or device identifiers are included. The App only reads rates from these services; it never sends any of your data to them.

Transaction Detection from Notifications

The App offers an optional feature that recognizes payments from the notifications posted by your banking and payment apps, so you can confirm a transaction instead of typing it in. This feature is off by default. It works only after you explicitly grant the App Android’s “Notification access” permission in your system settings and pick which apps it may watch. You can turn it off, or revoke the permission, at any time.

While the feature is on, the App reads the title and body text of notifications posted by the apps you selected, and ignores every other notification on your device. Reading and interpreting that text happens entirely on your device. The recognized text is never sent to the Developer, and it is never sent to any third party.

From a matching notification, the App keeps only the fields it needs to propose a transaction: the amount, the currency, whether money went out or came in, the merchant name, the last four digits of the card if the notification shows them, the time the notification was posted, and the package name of the app it came from. The raw notification text itself is deliberately not stored. These pending items live in the same local database as the rest of your data and are removed once you confirm or dismiss them; they are excluded from the App’s manual JSON export.

The App recognizes most notifications using a list of known bank notification formats that is bundled inside the App itself — no network request is made to match them. When those patterns do not recognize a notification, the App falls back to Google’s ML Kit Entity Extraction to locate an amount in the text. ML Kit runs its model locally on your device; Google Play services may download that model to your device once, but the notification text is analyzed on the device and is not uploaded.

When a payment is recognized, the App posts a local notification on your device so you can review it. That notification never leaves your device.

obleek Pro Subscription

The App offers an optional paid subscription, obleek Pro. If you choose to subscribe, the purchase itself is processed by Google Play Billing. The Developer never sees or receives your payment-card details; Google handles the payment under Google’s Privacy Policy.

To determine whether your subscription is active — including across a reinstall or a new device, via “Restore purchases” — the App uses RevenueCat, a third-party subscription-management service. RevenueCat receives information about the purchase itself (such as the product purchased, its status, and the Google Play purchase receipt) together with an anonymous identifier it generates for the installation and basic technical information such as the device model, operating-system version, App version, and country. The App does not ask you for a name, email address, or any other personal identifier, does not send one to RevenueCat, and does not enable RevenueCat’s collection of device advertising identifiers. No transaction, account, or other financial data from the App is ever sent to RevenueCat. RevenueCat’s handling of this data is described in its privacy policy.

You manage or cancel a subscription through your Google Play account, not through the Developer.

Usage Analytics

The first time you open the App, you are asked whether to allow anonymous usage analytics. This is opt-in — the App works the same either way, and you can change your choice at any time in Settings → Privacy.

If enabled, this is handled by Google’s Firebase Analytics and Firebase Crashlytics. The App reports only anonymous, structured events describing which screens and features were used — for example, that a screen was opened, that a transaction of a given type was added, that transaction detection was switched on or off, that a payment notification was recognized, or that the subscription screen was shown, a plan was selected, or a purchase completed, was cancelled, or failed.

These events never include amounts, account names, categories, tags, notes, merchant names, card digits, or any other content from your transactions or from the notifications the App reads. The one identifier an event may carry about another app is the package name of a notification source you chose to watch (for example, your bank’s app), which is reported so the Developer can tell which banks need better recognition.

If you allow analytics, anonymous crash reports (for example, a stack trace and basic device information) may also be sent to help fix bugs. None of this is tied to your name, email, or any other personal identifier, because the App has no accounts or sign-in of any kind, and the App explicitly removes the Android advertising-identifier permission, so no advertising ID is collected.

Backup & Export

Android’s built-in device backup may include the App’s local database as part of your regular phone backup (for example, to your Google Account), the same way it backs up other apps on your device. Your app-lock PIN and your theme preference are explicitly excluded from this backup and never leave your device.

You can also manually export your data to a JSON file, or import a previously exported file, from within the App. This is entirely under your control — the file is written to (or read from) a location you choose on your device, and is never transmitted anywhere by the Developer.

App Lock

If you choose to enable the optional app lock, the PIN you set is stored on your device only as a salted, one-way hash — never in plain text — and is never transmitted anywhere. If your device supports it, you may instead unlock the App using your device’s biometric authentication (fingerprint or face); this is handled entirely by your device’s operating system, and no biometric data is ever seen by the App or the Developer.

Data Sharing

The Developer does not share, sell, or transmit your financial data to third parties, because that data never leaves your device in the first place. The only outside services the App talks to are:

  • the public exchange-rate providers described above;
  • Google Play services, for the on-device text-recognition model used by transaction detection, if you enable that feature;
  • Google Play Billing and RevenueCat, if you purchase or restore an obleek Pro subscription;
  • Firebase Analytics and Crashlytics, only if you opt in to analytics.

None of these receive your transactions, balances, notes, or the contents of the notifications the App reads.

Children’s Privacy

The App does not knowingly collect any information from anyone, including children under 13.
